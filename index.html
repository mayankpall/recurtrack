The quiet leak

Recurring money is the money nobody watches.

It leaves on its own schedule, in amounts small enough to ignore, from an account you only check when something feels wrong. And the share your flatmate owes you? That is a conversation nobody wants to start twice.

₹3.8L
What a fairly ordinary Indian household commits to recurring payments in a year.

₹15,000
The RBI cap above which an auto-debit needs your approval every single cycle.
How it works

Three things, done properly.

01

Everything you pay, in one list

Tap a service and it fills in the price, the cycle and how it debits — Netflix is three taps, not a form. Rent, the maid, the newspaper and the gym sit in the same list, because your money does not care which is a subscription.

A reminder lands before each one, and anything on AutoPay rolls its own cycle forward. You are never asked to confirm a payment your bank already made.

02

Split it without the awkward conversation

Add whoever shares it and RecurTrack works out each person's rupee share. Type one amount and everyone you have not touched rebalances around it — the way you would expect, not the way spreadsheets do it.

Then it writes the message for you. Five tones, from breezy to firm, because asking your mother is not asking your flatmate.

03

Get the money back, over UPI

One tap sends a request with a UPI deep link attached. They open it, approve it in their own app, and the money goes straight from their bank to yours.

Owed groups by person, not by bill — one total, one ask, however many things they are in on. And it tells you who actually pays on time.

Privacy

It never connects to your bank. That is the design, not a setting.

Most money apps start by asking for read access to your account. RecurTrack cannot, because it never holds, receives or moves a rupee. Payments go straight between two UPI apps, and we take no cut and see no balance.

📱

Your records stay on your phone

Payments, people and splits live in a database on your device. Not on a server, not in an analytics pipeline.

🔗

No bank connection, ever

No account linking, no transaction scraping, no read access. We could not look at your balance if we wanted to.

👤

An account is optional

Start without one. Add an email later only if you want to get your list back on a new phone — it keeps everything.

☁️

Backup is off until you say so

Cloud backup and household sharing are opt-in. Nothing leaves the phone unless you switch them on, and the policy says exactly what happens when you do.

RecurTrack Plus

The free app is the whole app.

Tracking, splitting, asking people to pay, reminders, low-balance and mandate-cap warnings — all free, permanently. Plus is for the parts that cost us something to run, or that save you the work of remembering.

Backup & restore

Every payment back on a new phone. Off by default.

Share with your household

Send the joint payments to a flatmate. Yours stay yours.

Automatic chase-ups

It remembers who still owes, and nudges you — escalating, not repeating.

Who actually pays

How long each person really takes, and who needs asking twice.

What stays free, always: unlimited payments, unlimited people, unlimited splits and folders, every reminder, and the warnings that stop a mandate bouncing. We do not think it is right to charge for telling you that you are about to lose money.
